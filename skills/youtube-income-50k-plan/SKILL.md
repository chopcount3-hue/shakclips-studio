# Shak Clips: the $50,000 in 6 months plan

Use when the owner asks how to reach $50,000, when setting a monthly money target, or when deciding which income lever to work on this week.
Legend: **[V]** = checked on an official page on 2026-11-11 (see Sources). **[H]** = heuristic, source named. **[A]** = a planning assumption to replace with real numbers.
YPP thresholds, AdSense and India/US withholding tax are in `youtube-monetization-revenue`; this skill does the money math and the non-ad levers.

## 1. The window and the starting point
| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Window | 2026-11-11 → 2027-05-11 = **181 days** |
| Uploads | 1 long + 1 Short a day = **181 long videos** |
| Now | 7 subscribers, about 3,000 lifetime views, not in YPP |
| Goal | $50,000 gross in the window |
- Ads cannot start before YPP. The bar is 1,000 subs + 4,000 watch hours, review takes about a month **[V]**, and the bar doubles for applicants from 1 Feb 2027 **[V]**. So the earliest plausible ad window is **2–3 of the 6 months [A]**.
- RPM (what we'd actually be paid per 1,000 views) is after YouTube's cut **[V]**; the creator share of watch-page ads is 55% **[V]**. Planning range for a news-and-history mix with a US-leaning audience: **$2–6 [H]**, per `youtube-monetization-revenue`. The $8–15 Education figure in the source repo is for an established US education channel **[H: agricidaniel]**.

## 2. Ads alone: the arithmetic
Views needed = $50,000 ÷ RPM × 1,000.
| RPM **[H]** | Total long-form views needed | Views/day over 181 days | Views/day if only 90 days are monetized | Watch hours at 3:00 AVD **[A]** | × our lifetime views |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $2 | 25.0M | 138,000 | 278,000 | 1,250,000 h | 8,300× |
| $4 | 12.5M | 69,000 | 139,000 | 625,000 h | 4,200× |
| $6 | 8.3M | 46,000 | 93,000 | 417,000 h | 2,800× |
| $10 (optimistic) | 5.0M | 27,600 | 55,600 | 250,000 h | 1,700× |

**The verdict: $50,000 from ads in 6 months is not going to happen from 7 subscribers.** At the middle of the range the channel would need about 69,000 long-form views *every day* — and 625,000 watch hours, which is 156× the 4,000-hour bar it has not yet cleared — while earning nothing at all until YPP is approved. Even the optimistic $10 RPM needs 5 million views. There is no schedule, topic choice or thumbnail that turns 3,000 lifetime views into 12.5 million in 181 days.
What ads *are* realistically worth here: at a strong-but-plausible 2,000 views per long video **[A]** across 181 videos (362,000 views), $4 RPM gives about **$1,450 gross**, and only the slice after YPP approval is actually paid. Ads are the long game. Treat them as the prize for month 7 onward, not this window's income.

- Even the stretch column lands **about $14,000 short** of $50,000, and the stretch column is a full-time animation studio, not a YouTube channel.
- To actually clear $50,000 in 181 days, the realistic shape is roughly **56 client videos at $900 each** — about 2.2 delivered every week for 26 weeks **[A]**. That is a job, done alongside the daily run. Say this plainly to the owner rather than implying the channel will produce it.
- So: the goal is achievable only as a **services business whose portfolio is the channel**. Everything below is ranked by how fast it turns into cash.

## 4. Lever (a): sell custom explainer videos — fastest to cash
Why first: paid on delivery, needs no subscribers, and our engine already renders 8–10 minutes a day.
**Packaging** (fixed scope, fixed price, 2 revision rounds):
| Tier **[H]** | Deliverable | Price | Build time **[A]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spark | 30–45 s vertical explainer, 1 revision | $150–300 | 3–4 h |
| Standard | 60–90 s explainer, script + AI voice + captions | $400–800 | 6–8 h |
| Premium | 2–3 min with charts, maps and custom palette | $900–2,000 | 12–16 h |
| Retainer | 4 Standard videos a month | $1,200–3,000/mo | ~1 day/week |
All prices are **[H]**: no official rate card exists. Start at the bottom of each band, raise it after every 3 accepted projects, and never cut the price below the band — give less scope instead.
**Who buys [A]:** edtech and course sellers, B2B SaaS "how it works" videos, agencies that need white-label animation, non-profits and school publishers, YouTubers who want an animated segment.
**Where to find them, legally:**
- Freelance marketplaces: Upwork's freelancer service fee is **0–15% per contract** and is fixed once the proposal is sent **[V]**. Fiverr is commonly cited at 20% of seller revenue **[H: Fiverr ToS summaries; the official page wasn't opened]**. On Upwork, asking to be paid off-platform violates the terms and can cost the account; contact details may be shared only after a contract starts **[V]**.
- LinkedIn: post one build-in-public clip a week, then message people who engage. LinkedIn prohibits bots, scrapers and automated messaging tools **[H: LinkedIn help on prohibited software; the page blocks fetching]** — so send every message by hand.
- Cold email, within **CAN-SPAM** **[V]**, which covers B2B email and is not limited to bulk: truthful headers and subject line, say clearly that it's an ad, include a valid postal address, give a working opt-out that keeps working for at least 30 days, honour opt-outs **within 10 business days**, and don't contract the duty away. Penalties run to **$53,088 per email** **[V]**. Cap it at 20 hand-written emails a day **[A]**, one follow-up, then stop.
- Portfolio = the channel, plus 3 unlisted 30-second samples (edtech, SaaS, history) and a one-page price list.
**Before any payment:** half up front, half on delivery; written scope naming the number of revisions; our house rules apply to client work too (no third-party photos, no edited real faces, no misleading claims). Decline crypto, gambling, "guaranteed returns" and medical-claim briefs.

## 5. Levers (b)–(e), in order
- **(b) Sponsorships.** Don't pitch below about 5,000 average views per long video **[H]**. Rates of **$10–30 per 1,000 expected views** are a heuristic **[H: agricidaniel]**; the media kit asks for the brand's budget first. Media kit: 1 page — who watches, last 10 videos' views, audience country mix from Analytics, 3 integration formats, price, contact. Once in YPP, Creator Partnerships in Studio → Earn gives a media kit and inbound brand inquiries; it needs YPP, age 18+, no strikes, and a listed country (India and the US are both listed) **[V]**. Disclosure is mandatory: tick paid promotion in Studio **[V]** *and* say it aloud and on screen **[V]** — the FTC treats a platform tool alone as not enough, and US law applies when US viewers are foreseeable **[V]**.
- **(c) Affiliate and YouTube Shopping.** Needs YPP, is unavailable to music and Made-for-Kids channels, and pays through AdSense 60–120 days after purchase **[V]**; India and the US are both listed **[V]**. Amazon Associates requires "As an Amazon Associate I earn from qualifying purchases", plus a clear per-link disclosure **[V]**. Realistically months 5–6, and small.
- **(d) Digital products.** Teacher packs (worksheet + timeline + quiz per history video) at $3–9 **[H]**, and Canvas-engine or thumbnail templates. Needs clickable description links, i.e. advanced features **[V]**. Platform cuts are **[H]**: Gumroad and Payhip both publish per-sale fees — read their current pricing pages before pricing anything.
- **(e) Licensing clips to education platforms.** Highest value per asset, slowest cycle (a quarter or more **[A]**). Only license work we made entirely ourselves, non-exclusively, and keep the YouTube version up. Needs a written licence; have a professional read the first one.

## 8. Kill and scale rules
- **Scale** a lever when it clears its target 2 months running: raise prices 20%, or add a retainer slot.
- **Kill** a channel of outreach after 100 personalised messages with 0 replies **[A]** — rewrite the offer, don't send more.
- **Kill** a product after 6 weeks with under 10 sales **[A]**.
- **Don't kill** the daily upload run. It is the portfolio, the YPP progress and the sponsor proof.
- If by **2027-01-10** the projected hours at 24 Jan are still under 4,000, drop the YPP deadline (that rule lives in `youtube-monetization-revenue`) and move the effort to client work. Chasing ads with spam costs the channel.

## 9. Red lines
- Never promise or imply guaranteed income — not to the owner, not to a client, not in a video. Every figure here is labelled.
- No bought views, subscribers, likes or comments, and no sub4sub. These break the fake-engagement policy and can end the channel **[V]**.
- No inflated numbers in a media kit or invoice, no fabricated case studies, no "as seen on" claims we can't prove.
- No undisclosed paid promotion, and no promoting a product we haven't used **[V]**.
- No reused third-party footage, no web photos of real people, no edited real faces — in client work either.
- No unsolicited email outside CAN-SPAM; no scraped lists; no automated LinkedIn tools.

## 10. Getting paid from India (general, not advice)
- Routes **[H]**: the marketplace's own withdrawal to an Indian bank account, or PayPal, Wise or a direct bank/SWIFT transfer for direct clients. Compare the fee and FX spread per route before invoicing; each platform's own help pages are the source.
- AdSense: payout threshold **$100** **[V]**; file the US tax info and claim the India treaty (15% instead of 30%) **[V]** — details in `youtube-monetization-revenue` §4.
- Invoices for foreign clients, export-of-services and GST treatment, advance tax, and any FEMA or bank documentation are **questions for a chartered accountant**. Ask one before the first big invoice, and keep every invoice and bank advice.
