Somewhere in the solar system there is a rock about 2.5 kilometres across that turns all the way around in just 4.18 minutes. Asteroid 491384, found in early data from the Vera C. Rubin Observatory, is the largest ultra-fast rotator known so far, spinning more than 30 times faster than the "speed limit" that almost every big asteroid obeys.

Most asteroids that size are rubble piles that would fly apart at that speed. So what is holding this one together, and what would happen to you if you tried to stand on it? We build it to scale, land on it, and look at the clues: the strength it needs, the sunlight that may have spun it up, and what is still unconfirmed.
